CANONICAL CONTRIBUTION RULES

The component contract.

The adopting project's canonical requirements and contracts remain authoritative. CVAS provides a reusable envelope for contribution and verification.

Authority and semantics

  1. The adopting project's canonical requirements and contract are authoritative.
  2. Every requirement is a verification target. Do not silently exclude a requirement.
  3. A function that returns a status without performing the declared work is not an implementation.
  4. A test is meaningful only when it calls the implementation and detects incorrect or missing behaviour.
  5. Missing, invalid, ambiguous or unverified inputs must follow the declared failure policy. Where the project uses HOLD, missing information must not become OK.
  6. Canonical semantics and contracts are independent of UI, simulator, AI provider, language and deployment environment.
  7. Do not claim a capability, fidelity, qualification or assurance level not established by evidence.
  8. A contributor's identity, experience, reputation or AI provider is not evidence of correctness.
  9. A skipped check is not a pass. A failed or skipped mandatory check blocks acceptance.
  10. Components must declare dependencies, state ownership, authority and replaceability.

Contribution and assurance

  • Any human or AI agent may submit a bounded component.
  • The pipeline validates the package, executes declared tests and produces an evidence report.
  • Risk classification is reviewed by policy; a contributor cannot self-lower the required assurance.
  • Class C changes require complete human examination of the change set and two independent approvals under the adopting repository's branch policy.
  • The harness does not merge, certify, sign or deploy.
  • Components must be removable or substitutable without losing historical provenance.

Minimum 110% testing rigor

The phrase 110% is a project policy threshold, not a numeric code-coverage claim. At minimum, the assurance case must address expected behaviour and independently derived expected results; missing, malformed and boundary inputs; failure and recovery behaviour; invariants and non-mutation/state ownership; compatibility and dependency impact; regression and adversarial cases appropriate to risk; reproducibility, provenance and explicit skipped-check reporting.

Project-specific extension is mandatory. Additional domain-specific checks must be added by the adopting project. Passing this generic harness alone does not establish domain correctness or safety.
